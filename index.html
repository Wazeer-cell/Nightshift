What I learned about darkness

The darkness matters more than the mask.

I’m not a scientist. But when nothing worked, I read everything I could find about why light was stealing my sleep. Three things stayed with me.

  • It protects your melatonin

    Light tells your brain it’s daytime and shuts melatonin down. True darkness lets the hormone of sleep flow freely again.

  • It deepens the restful sleep

    Uninterrupted darkness lets you sink into slow-wave sleep — the deep, restoring kind that clears your head overnight.

  • It quietens the whole body

    With nothing to see, your body slips into rest — heart rate softens, and you finally let go of the day.

An educational summary of how light and darkness affect sleep — not a medical or product-specific performance claim.

Engineered, not stitched

Three layers, thinner than a whisper.

Most masks ask how to make it softer. I asked how thin it could be while blocking every last trace of light. The answer was three quiet layers.

  • The outer shell

    An athletic nylon-and-spandex finish that breathes and moves like a second skin — never a hot, heavy trap.

  • The blackout core

    A dedicated middle layer that ends the light without adding bulk. This is the whole point of Nightshift.

  • The skin layer

    Soft, cool, moisture-wicking. The side that touches you — no clamminess, no morning creases.

All of it, just 1–2mm thin.
